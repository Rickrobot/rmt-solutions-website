--------------------------------------------------------------------------
The "CPA hire vs contract lift" SERP was the densest commercial SERP found in
the entire review — nine dedicated pages from Ainscough, Sparrow, Synergy,
Citylifting, AE Engineering, AR Banks, Cadman, Lift Minicranes and CraneQuote.

Every single one of them is a crane hire company.

That matters because the question underneath it — "do I need to supply my own
Appointed Person?" — has an answer that determines whether the reader buys a
cheaper hire package or a more expensive contract lift. Not one independent
voice is present in those results, and the reader at that exact moment is
precisely your customer: someone who has just realised the planning duty might
be theirs and does not know what to do about it.

HOW IT IS WRITTEN — PER YOUR STEER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
NEUTRAL EXPLAINER. The post nowhere says or implies that crane hire companies
are conflicted, biased, or not to be trusted. It states the position factually
and lets the reader draw their own conclusion.

That was your call and it is the right one commercially — if a crane hire firm
who refers you work reads this page, there is nothing in it to object to. It
also happens to be the more quotable version, which helps it get linked to.

DUTIES ONLY, NO COSTS. No price comparison, no day rates, no figures for
contract lift vs hire. The one place money is mentioned is a neutral
observation that the contract lift rate is higher because it includes planning
and risk transfer — framed as an explanation, not a sales point. Nothing in the
post can date, and nothing can be wrong about somebody else's pricing.

The post does make one commercial argument, in a section called "the third
option most people miss": that CPA hire plus an independent Appointed Person is
a legitimate middle path between the two standard options. That is stated
plainly and then immediately qualified — it says outright that complex, blind
or high-consequence lifts are often better placed with a contract lift
provider, and that "an honest AP will tell you when that is the case". Being
seen to turn work away is what makes the rest of the page credible.


FACTUAL BASIS — CHECKED, NOT ASSUMED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
The legal claims were verified against primary sources rather than written from
memory, because getting a clause reference wrong on a page like this would be
worse than not publishing it.

  - CPA Model Conditions clause 8(a): an operator supplied by the owner is
    "regarded as the servants or agents of the Hirer" for all purposes
    connected with working the plant, and the Hirer is "solely responsible for
    all site costs and claims arising in connection with the operation of the
    Plant". Verified against the CPA Crane Hire Terms PDF hosted by Ainscough.
  - CPA Model Conditions clause 13(b): the Hirer is liable for loss of or
    damage to the Plant during the hire period. Same source.
  - CPA Supplementary Conditions for Mobile Cranes: "BS 7121 makes it clear
    that the Hirer is in overall control of the lifting operation and has
    responsibility for providing a suitably trained and experienced 'appointed
    person'." Same source.
  - LOLER 1998 Regulation 8 — planning by a competent person.

Ricky, please read the clause references before this goes live. They are
correct as far as the published 2021 CPA crane hire terms go, but you use these
conditions in practice and I do not — if any of it reads wrong to you, say so
and I will change it.
